# Interview Prep

Reference library for interview prep — etiquette, answer structures, and a question bank to draft answers against. When a specific interview is coming up, copy the relevant questions into that job's note (under Interview Notes in [[Templates/Job Application|the Job Application template]]) and fill them in for that role specifically.

## Timing & Format Basics

- **In person:** arrive 15–20 min early with your notes, CV and something to drink. Use the bathroom once more before you go in.
- **Virtual:** log on 10–15 min early (tech issues happen), have notes/CV ready, sit in a distraction-free space, keep a drink nearby. Bathroom break before you start.
- **Per answer: 3–5 minutes max.** Don't be afraid to ask permission to give an example, or offer one.
- **Don't know an answer?** Ask for clarification, or take a few seconds to collect your thoughts and answer as best you can. You can always follow up by email afterwards with a better answer.

## Interview Types

- **Recruiter / alignment call** (15–20 min) — a quick gauge of fit. Typical questions: which role(s) suit you best, how long you've been unemployed, notice period, Right to Work in the UK, recent DBS check, where you live (commute check), expected salary/range.
- **Practical interview** (30/45/60/90 min) — Stage 1 assessment/test → Stage 2 presentation/project + interview → Stage 3 panel interview.

Practical interviews break into 5 sections: **General** (tell me about yourself + role/employer alignment + strengths/weaknesses) → **Strength-Based** → **Technical** → **Behavioural (STARR)** → **Wrap-Up**.

| General (Tell Me About Yourself) | 1 | 5 min |

## AI Mock-Interview Workflow

1. Open your AI assistant of choice and ask it to act as an interview coach.
2. Paste in your specialised CV (personal summary through qualifications) — **leave out name, location, email, LinkedIn, GitHub and portfolio links**.
3. Paste in the job description (or link).
4. Ask it to generate 40–60 Stage 1/2/3 questions for that role, split as above (1 general / 3 strength / 3 technical / 3 STARR).
5. Practice daily, ~30 min/session.

## Interview Etiquette Checklist

### Pre-Interview
- [ ] Research the company (mission, values, products, special projects, recent news, achievements)
- [ ] Read the job description and map your required/desirable skills against it
- [ ] Research the interviewer(s) on LinkedIn and the company website
- [ ] Print all materials: job description, CV, cover letter, references, portfolio, certificates
- [ ] Bring all materials + a notebook with key structures/formulas + 2 pens
- [ ] Practice key questions using the structures below (Hook/Past/Present/Future + STARR)
- [ ] Plan responses to common questions (strengths, areas for improvement, achievements)
- [ ] Prepare your own questions for the interviewer (see Wrap-Up section)
- [ ] Confirm interview details: time, date, format, location, expected participants
- [ ] **Virtual:** test camera/mic/internet/meeting link; choose a quiet, well-lit space with a neutral background
- [ ] **In person:** plan your route, arrive 10–15 min early, dress appropriately, bring CV copies + notepad
- [ ] Get rest, arrive calm and confident, phone off/silent, bathroom before you start

### During Interview
- [ ] Greet staff/interviewers politely, smile, confident posture
- [ ] Maintain open body language and balanced eye contact (engaging, not staring)
- [ ] Pause briefly before answering to compose your thoughts
- [ ] Keep answers concise and focused — don't ramble
- [ ] Back up responses with clear examples using STARR
- [ ] If you don't know something, be honest and express willingness to learn
- [ ] Never speak negatively about yourself, past roles, or past employers
- [ ] Keep tone/volume professional and enthusiastic
- [ ] Engage with thoughtful follow-up comments or clarifying questions
- [ ] On salary: stay flexible — *"I'm open to negotiating depending on the overall package and responsibilities."*
- [ ] Ask your prepared questions, and **always ask what the next steps are**

### Post-Interview
- [ ] Send a thank-you email **within 24 hours** to each interviewer — *"Thank you for the opportunity to interview for [Role Name]."*
- [ ] Reference specific discussion points where relevant
- [ ] If you fumbled a question, use the email to give a better answer
- [ ] Attach anything they asked for (references, follow-up material, slides)
- [ ] Write notes on what went well and what to improve next time
- [ ] If no response after 7–10 days: *"I've applied on DD/MM and interviewed on DD/MM. I'd like to check on the status of my application, interview feedback and next steps as I'm still very interested in this role."*
- [ ] If declined: thank them for the opportunity and ask for feedback
- [ ] Stay professional and courteous regardless of outcome — every interview is a learning experience

## General Questions — Hook / Past / Present / Future

**Formula:**
- **Intro/Hook** (10–30s) — a short storytelling opener about your passion
- **Past** (30–45s) — academic/professional experience that prepped you for this role
- **Present** (1–2.5 min) — alignment with employer (specific research) *and* alignment with required/desirable skills
- **Future** (10–25s) — how you'll grow within the role/organisation

**Alignment with employer** — name something specific and researched: a project, value, or initiative. *"I was attracted to [Company] because I'd like to work on [specific project], which only a handful of employers are currently investing in within this industry."*

**Alignment with role** — for each required skill, just **name it** (skill + where you got it), no examples needed here: *"I gained prompt, professional stakeholder handling as an Engineer."* Do the same for desirable/recommended skills.

Two things to keep in mind: (1) this is your chance to share who you are as an individual and as a professional — passions, credentials, development; (2) "in-group" — do they see you fitting their team? Tell a story, but keep it to bullet points of what you want to convey, minus recent hobbies.

## Strength & Technical Questions — mini-STARR (A·R·R)

Shorter version of STARR — skip straight to what you did:
- **A** — what actions did you take
- **R** — what was the result
- **R** — upon reflection, what did you learn

Draft each of these using A-R-R:
- Most successful / most challenging project — and why (4-part question: successful, challenging, why×2)
- Tools/software you're most competent in, relevant to the role, and why
- How you keep up to date with industry trends (continuous learning / LinkedIn groups / conferences / network)
- Your teamwork / leadership style — cover both "no leader without a team" and "no team without a leader," and consider psychological safety
- What you'd do if you knew you were going to miss a deadline (tests accountability, problem-solving, honesty under pressure)
- What you owe an employer, and what they owe you (work ethic, expectations, loyalty)
- How you like to receive praise/feedback
- Something you strongly believe that most people disagree with (independent thinking, conviction)
- How you'd teach something complex in 5 minutes (communication, clarity, patience)
- What you do when you completely disagree with your manager (conflict resolution, emotional intelligence)
- How you handle working with someone you don't like (professionalism, separating emotions from work)
- A difficult decision that disappointed people (ethical reasoning, leadership)
- Any work gaps — when, why, and what you did during them

## Behavioural Questions — Full STARR (S·T·A·R·R)

- **S — Situation:** the role/context you were in, and the problem needing resolution
- **T — Task:** your plan *before* acting — passive planning verbs (*plan, organise, identify, visualise, discuss, gather, research, map out*)
- **A — Action:** what you actually did — active verbs (*did, analysed, addressed, presented, calculated, communicated, designed*)
- **R — Result:** the outcome, tied back to the situation and tasks
- **R — Reflect:** *"Upon reflection, I learned…"* + what you'd do the same (if successful) or differently (if not) — this closing reflection is what makes you stand out; most interviewers don't expect anything after Results

Always answer in first person ("I"), even for team achievements. Spend roughly two-thirds of your time on Action–Result–Reflect.

### Worked Example — Managed a Difficult Situation
**S:** My sister was crying because she couldn't find her favourite shoes and was going to be late for school.
**T:** I (1) identified how to calm her emotions, (2) visualised where she'd last had the shoes, (3) planned an alternative pair in case they weren't found, (4) organised our time so she wouldn't be late.
**A:** I (1) addressed her emotions and calmed her down, (2) retraced her steps, (3) secured an alternative pair, (4) ensured she got to school on time.
**R:** She was elated, we found the shoes, and she got to school on time.
**R (reflect):** Upon reflection, I learned to always have a spare pair ready — I set up a "shubboard" (shoe cupboard) to store the family's shoes and prevent it happening again.

### Worked Example — Difficult Client
**S:** Working at [Company] as [Role], a client grew frustrated about project delivery delays, risking a complaint.
**T:** (1) Identify their concerns calmly and professionally, (2) plan an approach to rebuild trust through transparent communication, (3) organise the project to get it back on track without compromising quality.
**A:** (1) Arranged a meeting to listen actively, (2) acknowledged the frustration and clarified the delay's cause, (3) managed the concerns calmly, (4) kept communication transparent, (5) presented a revised plan with clear timelines/milestones, (6) sent regular progress updates, (7) coordinated with the team to prioritise critical deliverables.
**R:** The client regained confidence in delivery, the project completed within the revised timeframe, and the relationship — plus follow-on work — was secured.
**R (reflect):** Upon reflection, I learned the value of addressing issues early, staying transparent, and leading with empathy under pressure; it reinforced proactive communication and collaborative problem-solving.

### Behavioural Questions to Draft (S-T-A-R-R Each)
- Data analysis / gathering data for customer use / team output — result and learning (skills: awareness, learning, reflection, adaptability)
- Improved a process and convinced the team to adopt it — or a challenging team task (same skills)
- Generated and implemented a new idea on a technical/research project
- Received unexpected results on a project — response and impact (skills: adaptability, problem-solving)
- Explained a complex concept to a non-technical audience (skill: simplifying)
- Worked with a difficult team member / line manager / stakeholder (skills: collaboration, conflict resolution)
- Met a tight deadline — how you managed tasks (skills: time management, prioritisation)
- Led a team on a project — what went well, what didn't, why (skills: collaboration, conflict resolution)
- Made a significant mistake in analysis/research — how you handled it (skills: accountability, asking for support)
- Adapted quickly to unexpected project changes while keeping the team aligned

## Wrap-Up Questions to Ask Them

Always close with: **"What are the next steps?"** — every single interview, every stage.

**1st interview:**
- Could you tell me more about the team I'd be working with, and how this role contributes to their success?
- How does the organisation encourage innovation within the team?
- What are the biggest challenges the team/department is facing right now, and how might this role help?
- How would you describe the work culture, and what helps someone thrive here?

**2nd interview:**
- How does the organisation support professional development and career progression?
- What are your expectations for this role in the first six months?
- What systems/tools/technologies does the team use daily — any upgrades or changes planned?

**3rd / final interview:**
- What training will I receive during onboarding?
- Are there any exciting upcoming projects this role would contribute to?
- How does the organisation support work-life balance?
- Where do you see this team/department in five years?

## Extra Personal Reflection Questions

Not for the interview itself — worth reflecting on for your own growth and to sharpen your authentic answers elsewhere:
- If money weren't an issue, what job would you do?
- What was the last book/TV show/film you really enjoyed?
- What are your hobbies outside work?
- When did you last feel proud at work, and why?
- If you could change one past decision, what would it be?
